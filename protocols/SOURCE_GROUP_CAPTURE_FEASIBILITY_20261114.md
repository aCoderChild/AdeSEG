# Source-group and capture feasibility audit — 14 November 2026

This is a feasibility and leakage audit, not a frozen confirmatory split or a claim of patient independence. No new model was trained or existing study changed. No validation/test mask files were opened; previously produced aggregate scores and prompt-label status were reviewed. The original automatic detector's training exposure remains unknown. Previously inspected seq8, seq11, seq20 and seq23 remain out of training.

## Findings

- `data/polypgen_sequence.jsonl` identifies sequence and relative image/mask path, but has no patient, examination or authoritative source-video identifier. The supplied dataset `readme.md` and [PolypGen dataset paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9902556/) describe positive sequences, empty masks and centre-based grouping; neither source establishes the numeric filename-prefix meaning or gives a patient map for these sequences. A filename group is a conservative leakage screen, not proof of source or patient identity.
- The current 39-read training set contains ten seq6 examples and the 10-read validation set contains five seq10 examples. Seq6 image names have prefix `2_endocv2021_positive_`, indices 1086–1478; seq10 names have the same prefix, indices 919–1079. Their ranges are disjoint but adjacent-looking. `negativeOnly/seq3_neg` uses that same positive-source-style prefix, indices 28–371. Treat all three as one suspected source group unless the provider establishes otherwise. Their exact relationship is unverified.
- Additional shared prefixes and ranges are seq1/seq2 (`0`, 69–327 / 927–1194), seq3/seq4 (`1`, 262–343 / 357–529), and seq7/priority seq8 (`3`, 44–282 / 724–1102). Seq9 has prefix `7` and no other local positive sequence with that particular naming pattern. Priority seq11 uses an `EndoCV2021_seq11_Image_` stem; seq20 and seq23 use distinct `seq5_C6_` and `seq8_C6_` stems. Different names do not establish different patients or videos.
- The earlier mixed-manifest duplicate audit checked exact bytes between all seq6/13 images and seq8/9/10/11/20/23 images, not decoded images, neighboring frames, same videos or patients. Visual appearance similarity is not sufficient evidence of source identity either. Future preflight must combine exact bytes, decoded-pixel hashes and vetted near-duplicate/source checks, and retain uncertain groups together.
- Automatic detector/point prompts and prediction trajectories in `lean_auto_multiscale_v1/cohort_combined_attempt01` exist for seq2–6 and seq8–23, not seq1/7. Existing seq6/13 prompt and full-trajectory observer replays were verified but use a project token-window variant; snapshots contain early FPN and decoder head inputs, not complete memory-attention reads. The current preflight explicitly hardcodes seq6/13. A wider capture requires a newly versioned manifest, generalized runner and independent checks; existing run receipts must not be edited or represented as new captures.
- Seq9/10 have already been used repeatedly for fixed-read validation. Other videos including priority seq11/20/23 and the C6 cohort have been inspected in earlier analyses and comparisons. Thus a new sequence-disjoint development partition can reduce leakage, but cannot become a previously untouched final test by renaming it. The one-shot YOLO checkpoint's training data are unknown; this limits detector-independent claims even for a disjoint refiner split.
- The paper notes negative-only video samples may be taken from confirmed normal examinations or from areas away from a polyp. These are labeled-negative **frames**, not proof the patient had no polyp, and centre/patient linkage is incomplete. Automatic one-shot initialization can fail to create an object in such a video; record detector misses rather than fabricating a ground-truth prompt. Empty annotated masks in positive videos should be called labeled empty, not clinically proven polyp-free frames.

## Conservative feasible development partition

For a fresh, explicitly exploratory run, one option is training on seq2, seq3, seq4, seq5, seq12, seq13 and seq14, treating seq3/4 as one suspected source group; validation on seq9 and seq15; keep seq8, seq11, seq20, seq23 and other C6 videos out of training. Exclude seq6, seq10 and negativeOnly/seq3_neg for now because they share the suspected `2_endocv2021_positive_` source group and seq10 has been used for validation. Exclude seq7 because of its shared prefix with priority seq8. Seq1 shares the prefix with seq2 and has no archived automatic run.

There are seven training sequence directories but at most six tentative filename groups: seq2; seq3/4; seq5; seq12; seq13; seq14. Some of the latter may still share a patient or examination; no such independence is asserted. Seq9 and seq15 have different recorded filename patterns from these training sequences and each other, but they too have been observed in previous project-wide analyses. Freeze the split before new captures. A genuinely patient-independent study needs a verified patient/examination mapping or an independently acquired dataset with patient IDs and documented detector pretraining. Protect a new unseen centre/patient cohort for final assessment rather than reusing any of these inspected development videos.

Archived prompts occur at frame indices seq2=3, seq3=0, seq4=9, seq5=0, seq12=0, seq13=7, seq14=0, seq9=5 and seq15=0. Initial masks and preprocessing must be frozen and hashed per sequence; verify the prompt stage on MPS and note any difference from CPU archived masks. A one-time prompt schedule is retained; do not use ground truth as inference prompt, redetection, an inference-time gate or a new pruning rule.

## Minimum practical engineering and training experiment

1. Register the above candidate split and video groups, and predeclare up to 25 equally spaced post-prompt capture indices per training sequence (seq3 has only 14 post-prompt frames), and 25 per validation sequence. Selection depends only on frame index and available prompt, not labels, predictions or failure scores. Freeze paths, frame order, prompt/mask, model, source hashes, device, resized image and sample indices before any new label access.
2. Reuse the existing observer and prompt pipeline through a separately versioned generalized runner. For each video run an MPS reference and observed trajectory with the same automatic prompt and fixed policy, verify full masks, memory events and positions match, and ensure the captured early features and native head inputs match their originals. Record all missed prompts and failures, including frames before initialization. Use float32, disable MPS fallback/fast math, and run a real CPU/MPS value/gradient and resized-mask parity gate before training. If the prompt or device changes versus prior studies, record it rather than relabeling historical results.
3. The candidate training videos contain 1,099 available post-prompt frames: 953 labeled-present and 146 labeled-empty when evaluated with the project's JPEG mask loader. These counts were derived from **training labels only** during this audit; they are not an instruction to use labels for sampling. The 164 possible fixed training captures and 50 validation captures remain highly correlated within videos. Report per-video and patient-group caveats. Consider negative-only captures in a later predeclared study only after source/centre audit and automatic-initialization feasibility checks.
4. Start from fresh refiner parameters, not any checkpoint selected on seq9/10. Compare a no-change captured baseline, same-capacity mask-only control and early/deep feature fusion with identical sample order, optimizer and seed; retain a no-fusion control and predeclare loss. Freeze all validation predictions before opening labels. Report per-video Dice, present-frame precision/recall, labeled-empty false alarms and pixel area, alongside the fraction of videos/frames that could not be prompted. One seed is an engineering pilot; repeat across seeds and independently held-out patients before any efficacy claim. Do not use ground-truth quantities in the inference path or change the object-presence/pointer decisions.
5. Do not launch a modified full-video system or claim it solves seq11/20/23 solely because fixed-read loss improves. The causal write-back and rollout require their own observer/no-op validation, matched complete trajectories and independent video assessment, and empty masks can be incomplete annotations.

## Resource estimate and limitations

Actual current MPS captures use approximately 8 MiB early features plus 33 MiB head snapshot per selected read. A fresh maximum 214-read capture therefore needs roughly 8.6 GiB for those tensors alone, before reference/observed masks, logs, archived checkpoint/provenance, any raw candidate dumps, cloud sync/cache and retry overhead. The Drive-mounted volume reported approximately 27 GiB available at audit time, subject to change. Do not stage new experiment artifacts outside the user-specified Drive output location; account for the provider's local sync/cache behavior.

For existing seq6/13 captures, first-to-last output-file modification times were about 54.5/55.9 seconds for 68 reference/observed frames and 204.5/226.6 seconds for 243 frames, respectively. These are rough file-timestamp spans, not controlled wall-clock benchmarks. Two full passes for the proposed 1,259 post-prompt train plus validation frames could plausibly take roughly 35–40 minutes at those measured rates, plus model startup, verification, I/O contention, training and retries. The estimate is not a guarantee and assumes the same hardware, frame rates and policy. The proposed new split has not been preflighted, captured, trained or scored.


## Safe-reuse-first amendment

The explicit 25-point target grid intersects 22 historical training reads: five seq2, five seq3 and eight seq4 CPU captures, and four seq13 MPS captures. Three intersecting seq4 reads were historically selected after labels were examined; their history must be disclosed, despite membership in the present deterministic grid. Reuse is **conditional**, not yet approved: require per-sequence automatic prompt and full-trajectory identity and matching feature/head validation, including explicit CPU/MPS discrepancy checks before mixing devices. Preserve source/device hashes. The seq13 prior MPS reference may be reused in place of a new reference run only if the new observed trajectory exactly reproduces its prompt, runtime, masks and trace. The previous seq4-only CPU/MPS comparison is not proof for seq2 and seq3. Any unsupported comparison requires fresh MPS capture.

If all 22 reads can be safely reused, at most 192 new records would be needed; the final tensors alone would use about 7.7 GiB. Allow potential extra local Drive sync/staging duplication, output masks/logs, retries and at least 4 GiB margin. The CPU preflight requires at least 22 GiB free as a conservative readiness screen, not a promise of sufficient cloud quota or final storage. Recheck immediately before capture and do not start if capacity is inadequate. The present manifest/config does not authorize or implement GPU capture; a new generalized runner and independent tests remain required.
